# Product north star — why Ben Noach should exist

## Product thesis

**Ben Noach is the guided Tanakh reader for a person who wants to understand the Word of Hashem through the Jewish textual tradition without first becoming an expert user of Jewish libraries.**

The product is built for:

- a Ben Noach who wants a serious lifelong study environment;
- a non-Jew who wants to become a righteous gentile and needs a coherent path into authentic sources;
- a reader coming from a Christian Bible who knows many verses but not the Jewish textual context around them;
- a non-religious reader who wants to encounter Tanakh, Rashi, Chazal and the classical interpretive tradition without prior yeshiva vocabulary.

The product is not valuable because it has more texts than Sefaria. It is valuable because it gives the reader **the right first path through the texts**.

## The gap

### A generic Bible gives text without the Jewish interpretive architecture

A normal Bible app can provide a translation, search, highlights and reading plans. It usually cannot answer, in an authentically Jewish source-chain:

- Why is this verse read this way?
- Why does Rashi stop here?
- What textual difficulty is Rashi answering?
- Is Rashi using peshat, Midrash, Gemara, grammar, or a combination?
- Where did this explanation come from?
- Does this passage describe a universal principle, a Noahide obligation, Israel's covenantal context, or a specifically Jewish practice?
- Which parts should a Ben Noach understand as context rather than as obligations addressed to Israel?
- What should I read next if I want to understand the point rather than drown in references?

### Sefaria gives extraordinary depth but assumes textual literacy

Sefaria is the deep library. It exposes an enormous graph of texts and commentaries. A new reader often does not yet know:

- which commentator to open;
- why a commentator matters;
- what a source category means;
- how a Midrash differs from peshat;
- how a supercommentary relates to Rashi;
- which legal/covenantal material is addressed specifically to Israel;
- which source is directly relevant to Bnei Noach;
- when enough context has been acquired to continue reading.

Ben Noach should make Sefaria **more usable by teaching the reader how to arrive there**.

## Core promise

> **Read Tanakh. Understand the classical Jewish reading. Know where the source comes from. Know what is universal, what concerns Bnei Noach, and what belongs specifically to Israel's covenant. Go deeper whenever you choose.**

## The product wedge

The distinctive journey is:

**READ → UNDERSTAND → SOURCES**

### READ

A calm, beautiful, continuous Tanakh reader.

The first encounter is scripture, not interface.

The reader can:

- read Hebrew with niqqud and te'amim;
- read a clearly identified Jewish translation;
- switch translation/version when licensed alternatives exist;
- move chapter-by-chapter without learning a library interface;
- select a verse without leaving the reading context.

### UNDERSTAND

This is the primary product advantage.

A selected verse opens only the material that helps the current reader understand it.

Possible sections:

1. **Understanding the verse** — concise beginner context.
2. **Rashi** — source text/translation, explicitly identified.
3. **Why Rashi comments here** — the concrete textual question.
4. **Understanding Rashi** — elucidation grounded in classical supercommentaries.
5. **Where this comes from** — Midrash, Gemara, Tanakh cross-reference, grammar, etc.
6. **Words and terms** — terminology the user should not be expected to know already.
7. **For Bnei Noach** — only where a real Noahide/universal relevance has been established.
8. **Who is being addressed?** — when a passage is specifically covenantal to Israel, explain that boundary cleanly as context.
9. **Sources & provenance** — exact edition/version/license/source path.
10. **Continue in Sefaria** — handoff to the complete library.

### SOURCES

The user explicitly chooses depth.

This can expose:

- full Rashi segments;
- Siftei Chakhamim;
- Mizrachi;
- Gur Aryeh;
- Divrei David;
- Torah Temimah;
- relevant Midrash/Gemara;
- related Tanakh passages;
- lexical/morphological data;
- disagreements between classical authorities where materially useful;
- complete provenance and license information;
- outbound links into Sefaria.

## The Noahide lens is curation, not redaction

The source text remains intact.

The guided product classifies material so a Ben Noach does not have to infer applicability from a corpus written largely inside Israel's covenantal life.

Recommended content-scope taxonomy:

- `UNIVERSAL_CREATION` — creation, humanity, image of God, providence, moral order.
- `NOAHIDE_CORE` — direct Noahide law/source material.
- `EMUNAH` — unity of Hashem, knowledge of God, rejection of idolatry, foundations of faith.
- `TESHUVAH_PRAYER` — repentance, prayer, gratitude, dependence on Hashem.
- `JUSTICE_ETHICS` — justice, theft, violence, sexual morality, charity, truth, social order.
- `NATIONS_PROPHECY` — the nations, messianic future, universal knowledge of Hashem.
- `ISRAEL_COVENANT_CONTEXT` — material necessary to understand Israel's covenant, history, land, priesthood, mitzvot or national mission.
- `JEWISH_PRACTICE_SPECIFIC` — practice/halakhic detail addressed specifically to Jews; normally absent from beginner guided paths unless needed to understand the passage.
- `ADVANCED_ORAL_TORAH` — deeper material available only through deliberate source exploration and subject to the project's review policy.

A passage can have more than one tag.

### Default behavior

The default beginner route prioritizes:

- universal principles;
- Noahide obligations;
- emunah;
- teshuvah/prayer;
- justice/moral order;
- the nations in prophecy;
- classical commentary needed to understand the actual verse.

Jewish-only legal detail does not become the user's curriculum merely because it is adjacent in a source database.

When Israel-specific covenantal material is necessary to understand the verse, it is presented as **context**, clearly labeled and explained rather than removed or treated as a Noahide obligation.

## Why the user returns

The product should create durable value rather than one-time apologetic novelty.

### 1. It remembers where the reader is

Local-first, privacy-preserving:

- last passage;
- reading progress;
- saved passages;
- display preferences;
- optional local notes later.

No account is required for v1.

### 2. It continuously teaches the reading method

Over time the user learns:

- what Rashi is doing;
- what peshat means;
- what Midrash is doing;
- how Chazal are cited;
- why supercommentaries exist;
- how Jewish textual argument works;
- when a legal statement is universal and when it is covenant-specific.

The product succeeds when the reader becomes increasingly capable of using Sefaria and primary sources independently.

### 3. Guided pathways answer actual Noahide questions

Recommended first pathways after the Genesis 1:1–5 demonstrator:

#### Creation and Humanity
Genesis 1–11.

Questions:

- Who is man before Israel exists?
- What is the image of God?
- What is human responsibility?
- What changes after the Flood?
- What is the covenant with Noah?

#### What Hashem Requires of Humanity
Genesis 2, 4, 6–9 plus reviewed classical Noahide sources.

#### The One God
Selected Torah, Prophets and Writings passages centered on divine unity, sovereignty, creation and idolatry.

#### Teshuvah
Cain, Nineveh/Jonah, Psalms and prophetic passages, with reviewed Noahide guidance.

#### Justice and the Righteous Gentile
Biblical justice, moral agency, violence, theft and judicial order, connected to relevant Chazal/Rambam material.

#### The Nations in the Prophets
Isaiah 2 and 11; Micah 4; Zephaniah 3; Zechariah 8 and 14; related passages.

#### Reading Rashi for the First Time
A short interactive pathway teaching `dibbur hamatchil`, peshat, Midrash and supercommentary by using real verses rather than abstract definitions.

#### Coming from a Christian Bible
An optional textual-orientation route:

- Jewish book ordering;
- Hebrew names and canonical refs;
- translation differences;
- context and chapter boundaries;
- how to inspect a later theological reading only after seeing the base Jewish reading.

It begins with Tanakh, not polemics.

## High-value interaction ideas

These are product opportunities, not mandatory v1 clutter.

### “Why is this here?”

One tap explains why a selected Rashi/source was surfaced for this verse.

### “Who is this addressed to?”

A compact contextual control answers whether the passage is:

- humanity/universal;
- Noah/Bnei Noach;
- Israel as a nation;
- priests/Levites;
- a specific biblical person/group;
- prophetic future/nations.

This is more useful for the target user than generic study badges.

### “Source ladder”

A visual but restrained provenance ladder:

`Genesis 1:1 → Rashi → Siftei Chakhamim → project elucidation`

Each step is inspectable.

### “Hebrew word” inspector

Later, word-level interaction can expose:

- pointed Hebrew;
- lemma;
- basic morphology;
- concise lexical gloss;
- BDB entry;
- occurrences in Tanakh.

This should use open data such as OSHB/BDB rather than an opaque proprietary lexical service.

### “Read around it”

For verses frequently extracted from context, provide a one-tap context view that expands the surrounding chapter/section rather than immediately showing polemics.

### “What the classical reader notices”

A short editorial cue can identify the textual feature that generated commentary:

- unusual word;
- grammar;
- repetition;
- sequence;
- apparent contradiction;
- missing/extra word;
- cross-reference;
- Midrashic tradition.

This is especially useful for learning how Rashi reads.

## Product personality

The product should feel:

- exact;
- calm;
- confident;
- beautiful;
- intellectually serious;
- recognizably Jewish in textual structure rather than decorative cliché;
- welcoming to a non-Jew without diluting Jewish source identity;
- useful enough for a rabbi to inspect without embarrassment;
- elegant enough for a non-religious first-time visitor to keep reading.

## Visual product rule

**The page earns beauty through typography, whitespace, proportion, directionality, and source hierarchy.**

A screenshot with all optional controls closed should still look complete.

The Tanakh itself is the hero visual.

## Trust model

The public user should be able to answer at any time:

- What is the verse/source I am reading?
- Which edition/version is this?
- Who wrote this commentary?
- Is this wording the historical source or a modern explanation?
- What evidence supports the explanation?
- Is this Noahide guidance reviewed, draft, disputed, or simply contextual?
- Can I open the complete source elsewhere?

Trust should come from inspectability, not from branding claims.

## Success test

A first-time user who knows only a normal Bible should be able to open Genesis 1:1 and, within minutes, understand:

- what the Hebrew text is;
- what translation they are reading;
- who Rashi is;
- why Rashi comments on this verse;
- which part of Rashi concerns Israel's covenantal context;
- which universal theological principle is being discussed;
- where Rashi's reasoning/source chain comes from;
- how to inspect the complete material in Sefaria.

The experience should make the user want to continue to Genesis 1:2 rather than return to a generic Bible app or abandon the source graph as too difficult.

## Expansion rule

Every new feature must strengthen at least one of these advantages:

1. easier authentic reading;
2. clearer classical Jewish interpretation;
3. clearer Noahide applicability/context;
4. stronger provenance;
5. better transition from beginner to primary sources;
6. better long-form reading quality.

This is the durable reason for Ben Noach to exist.
